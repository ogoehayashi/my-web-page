Hello, world — again

A website doesn't have to be finished to be worth starting. What matters is having a place of your own to keep writing in.

Hi, I'm Coco. Until a few months ago my days were built around vouchers and working papers — I studied accounting (the ACCA track) at Chongqing University, and spent this spring as an audit intern at a firm in Chongqing. Now I'm in Singapore, in the GDipSA programme at NUS, learning to build software instead of checking books.

This is the first post on my site, and mostly it's a note to myself: don't wait until you feel ready. Lay the foundation first, and fill in the rest as you go.

Why a place of my own

Social platforms are convenient, but their shape is decided by someone else. There's a character limit, an algorithm deciding what gets seen, and whatever you write today is buried by tomorrow. A personal site is different: every colour, every margin, every word is mine to choose. That feeling is hard to replace.

Writing forces you to think clearly, because a vague sentence can't hide vague thinking.

What I'll write about

Roughly three things: what I'm learning as I move from accounting into software, the mistakes I make while building projects, and the occasional thought that doesn't fit anywhere else. I'm not aiming for a schedule — only for posts I'm happy to put my name on.

Auditing taught me something plain but useful: every number has to be traceable back to its source, and when something doesn't reconcile, you don't skip it — you go through it line by line. That habit turns out to be surprisingly good training for reading a stack trace at midnight.

Here's to a place that keeps growing.
